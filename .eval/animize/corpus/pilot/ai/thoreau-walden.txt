Most of us worry far more than our circumstances require. We could hand off a good share of the care we lavish on ourselves and lose nothing by it, since the world is built to accommodate our weak points as well as our strong ones. For some people, constant anxiety has become a chronic condition that no amount of reassurance seems to cure. Part of the problem is that we overrate the importance of our own work. Look at how much gets done without us, and think about what would happen if we were laid up for a month: things would mostly carry on. Yet we stay on guard all day, refusing to trust anything we cannot control, and only at night, worn out, do we reluctantly let go and accept that the next day is uncertain. We are so committed to living the way we live that we treat our habits as sacred and cannot imagine doing otherwise. This is the only way, we tell ourselves. But there are as many ways to live as there are directions to walk from a single point. Change looks like a miracle from the outside, and yet it is happening all the time, in small increments, to everyone. Real knowledge, as an old proverb puts it, is knowing what you know and knowing what you do not. And once one person has turned a hope into a fact, sooner or later everyone builds on it.
