The simplest way to understand what government is for is to imagine a situation without one. Picture a handful of people who have landed in some remote place, cut off from every other society. They stand in for the first inhabitants of any country. The first thing they will do, before anyone thinks about rules or leaders, is form a community, because none of them can manage alone. One person's needs run far ahead of one person's strength, and few people can stand extended isolation. Each of them will look for help from the others, and each will be asked for help in return. Consider the practical problem of building shelter. Four or five people working together can put up a decent cabin in a clearing within a season. A single person could spend an entire lifetime and never finish. He might cut down the trees, but he could not haul the logs, and if he somehow hauled them, he could not raise them into walls by himself. In the meantime he has to eat, so every day the work is interrupted by hunting or gathering, and every new need drags him off in another direction. If he falls ill or breaks a leg, he is finished, not because the injury itself would kill him but because it would stop him from doing the work that keeps him alive.
